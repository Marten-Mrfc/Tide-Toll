# game: the Godot project

Purpose: rendering, camera, UI and audio. Godot 4.7 .NET, C# only, Mobile renderer. The simulation lives in `../sim` (project reference, always built optimised), so the game never owns game rules: it reads the `World` through read-only spans and changes it only through commands.

The game is a book with two chapters and opens on the first:

- **Chapter I, "The Hamlet"** is Prototype 1 as the finished browser toy plays it (`SimParams.Default`, the river rules off): the same town as the toy for the same seed, villagers wearing trails into the page, fences, gates (at any angle), gravel, the footbridge and erase with a flow preview of the routes that would change (the library's `Describe` wording), rain that turns worn trails to mud until it dries, and cottages that new families build beside busy trails. No boats, no coin.
- **Chapter II, "The River"** is the river prototype (the same with `Main.River` on; the library then turns rain and growth off): the same path tools (gravel and gates cost nothing), plus boats, coin and the crossings (below).

The player turns the page with the chapter bookmark (the brick-red ribbon at the top of the tabs, "Chapter II ›" or "‹ Chapter I") or the C key: a leaf of paper turns over the book (`PageTurn`, about 1.2 s), the old town is held still from the first frame of the turn (no tick, house, boat or footstep under the leaf), the book is built again under it for the other chapter, a fresh town from the current seed (walker count, speed, pause, sound, preview, overlay and view kept), and the new pages are revealed. Reset and New map stay inside the chapter. Command line: `-- --chapter 1|2` (default 1); `-- --no-river` is Chapter I.

Both chapters have a wear overlay, pause and four speeds, reset and new map, 50 to 500 walkers, and basic sound, in the Living Map style with the Hamlet assets. The left page is the chapter page (title, how to play, the village facts, path health, the legend and the status line; Chapter I adds houses and the weather, Chapter II the ledger); the controls are paper bookmarks out of the map page's edge.

In Chapter II it plays the river prototype (Prototype 1, step 2): rowboats and masted barges on the simulation's schedule sail up to the landing, wait at crossings they cannot pass with their patience running down, and turn back when it runs out; the player buys a footbridge, ferry or drawbridge from bookmarks priced from the rules (`RiverRules`), works the drawbridge by hand or hires its keeper, and the chapter page keeps the ledger and a chronicle of margin notes. The seasons reach the river: a spring flood washes over the band beside it and closes the ford (then leaves mud), and winter ice freezes it (boats held, villagers crossing anywhere, their paths scratched into the ice until the thaw). See "The river" below.

## Chapter I: gravel, gates, rain and a growing town

- Gravel is free in both chapters. It is drawn in the ground shader as a pale stony strip (#EEEADC wash, stones #77796F that fade to their mean below a pixel, a 1 to 2 px rim #5A5C55): the toy's 1.15-cell stroke joining neighbouring gravel cells, so a strip along a slanting trail is one band. Uploaded only when the gravel grid changes.
- Gates are cut into the fence's rail where the toy cuts them and drawn with `fence-gate` standing ajar (0.32 rad) between two gate posts.
- Rain (`World.Weather`, `World.Mud`, Chapter I only): streaks over the map page while it rains; the land darkens with the wetness; mud cells are wet trail and dry from the least-worn first over the last `DrySimSec`; the chapter page says "Rain, day N", "Mud until day N" or "Dry"; a rain bed and wet footsteps from the existing sounds.
- Growth: `Buildings`, `Houses` and the walker count change during a tick; nothing caches their length. `SimDriver.HouseBuilt` tells the town (node, rising), the marks (yards and shadows rebuilt), the scenery (trees on the plot felled) and the overlay (`NewHouses`); the page says "Houses: N" and "newest on day N", and once the room the world keeps for grown houses is used up (`World.GrowthRoomFull`) the line adds "no room for more families" (`ChapterPage.RoomFullText`), once.

## The river (Chapter II)

`Main.River` (a `RiverRules`, default `RiverRules.Default`) is turned into `Main.Params.River` when the book is built for Chapter II; tests change it (for example a fuller purse) before adding the scene, with `Main.StartChapter = 2`, or before `SwitchChapter(2)`. In Chapter I `Main.Params.River` is null.

- Boats are drawn from `RiverView.Boats` every frame. The simulation's boat is a point at its bow row; the hull is drawn behind it, so a held barge stops short of the bridge. The simulation queues boats 2 rows apart; they are drawn nose to tail. Moored boats lie either side of the jetty. Boats grow to 1.6x at overview zoom.
- A raised leaf seen from straight above is only a dark edge, so the state is carried by shape on the map: the signal disc's arm (flat = walkers' turn, upright and blue = boats' turn), the open water under the lift, the barriers, the walkers bunched at the banks (waiting walkers are drawn spread round their stopping point), the badge's glyph and words.
- Riders are drawn on the raft as it is drawn (the sim moves them stage to stage, which is longer than the raft's travel).
- Events go through `RiverFeed` (one drain a frame). Strings are built only for notable events (margin notes, floats), never per frame.
- Reading at the play view (about 307 m of map in view): boats are drawn 2.2x long and as wide as the water allows, so a barge (long, mast and white yard) reads apart from a rowboat; each crossing type has its own map mark; patience rings and signal discs keep a fixed size on screen at every zoom; notes keep off boats and crossings; a selected drawbridge's badge keeps off its bridge and the place tags.
- The third pass (decisions B, C and D): grain carried through the ford is drawn wet over its carrier's head and sells for less (the landing's note, the ledger's "The ford" line, the Ford tag's second line); the drawbridge's keeper is earned by raising it by hand for waiting barges (the badge counts down, a raise with no boat waiting is said not to count, the hire is locked until then, a bell, a note and a glow at the moment); the starting purse shows on the price tabs; the flood band's outer edge is a faint dotted ink line on the dry map from the first day (`MapGround.BandMarked`, shader `band_mark`). Every price, count and share is read from `RiverRules`.
- Seasons: the simulation flips `RiverView.Flooded`, `Muddy` and `Frozen` (they do not bump `World.Version`). The ground eases toward them each frame (`MapGround.Flood`, `Mud`, `Ice`, 0..1): the flood's wash fills outward from the bank over about two seconds and ends in a dotted ink edge; mud darkens worn paths in the band; ice is pale with ink cracks, still water lines, and the ice-wear layer as whiter scratched paths. Boats stand still in the ice (the simulation freezes their clocks); the river loop drops 18 dB.

## Look and performance notes

- Ground ("the paths are the art"): the wear texture is read, never changed, through a lookup warped by noise (under half a cell, so no cell squares or vector strokes show), at three widths: a softened core, a wide spread and a broad average. The spread draws a wide, pale fringe of bent grass with strokes along the walking direction (the direction is the wear's slope, from the same samples). Bare soil is blended by height: the grass has a noise height and wear takes it down from the lowest tufts up, so a lightly used trail is a broken line of light, mottled soil through grass and only the middle of a busy one is solid. The darker packed brown is kept for trails both well worn and wide, and the hollow-way texture for the simulation's hollow flag. Paper grain on top. Lit with a custom `light()` so unshadowed ground is exactly the texture colour and shadows of houses and trees still fall on it. The field is drawn as crop rows in the shader (trampled to earth where trails cross); it reads better than the 16 m field model at the simulation's 18 x 12 m footprint.
- Villagers: a tunic in one of six palette colours (no greens, which vanish into the meadow), a cape a shade darker, a smaller head as a dark hood, straw hat or soot cap, a pale pack behind as the facing cue, a soft shadow disc toward the north-west, and an ink hull of 1.2 px up close thinning to 0.8 px at overview zoom.
- Town: each cottage has a soft drop shadow, an inked yard outline with a gap at its door and one of four thatch tones; the well has a round yard and the market a square. Paper tags name the Well, Market, Field and Ford at overview zoom (as the toy does) and fade out zoomed in.
- Trees: copses of 5 to 12 round the map's edge, a few small ones and lone trees inside, from the seed; crowns drawn 18% wider and 8% flatter and shaded as one ball each (round clumps from above), each a slightly different green.
- Draw calls in the play view: about 100 on seed 1 (ground 1, marks 1, walkers 3 with the hull and shadows, trees 3, reeds 1, ford stones 1, a mesh per building piece, ink 2, plus the book).
- Flow preview cost per frame is a hard cap: bounded predictor slices (`FlowPreviewer.SliceWork` = 1000 units, 0.8 to 1.4 ms at worst here) with the clock read before each, so a frame never spends more than its preview budget plus one slice. On 2026-11-25, ten perf runs in a row gave preview-run worst frames of 8.8, 11.4, 9.2, 10.7, 9.8, 9.4, 9.9, 9.5, 10.6 and 14.1 ms (gate 25 ms; before, an unbounded step could take 6 to 12 ms and the gate failed about 2 runs in 7).
- Per frame the game allocates nothing on the managed heap except the interface's text, rebuilt about twice a second and only where a value changed (10 to 17 bytes per frame on average with 500 walkers; 0 with `-- --no-hud`). A grown house costs about 2 KB once (its node, the marks mesh, the scenery's reed list reused): Chapter I's growth run at 8x measured 21.2 B/frame on 2026-11-25, down from 42.3 (the marks rebuild allocated two arrays per quad over every building, and the reeds a new list, for each house). A finished flow preview allocates its result and route mesh (a few kilobytes), which is not steady state.
- Interface: screen-space Controls (crisper than a SubViewport on the page), sized from one scale factor (window height / 1080, at least 0.78, fonts never under 12 px) and rebuilt on resize. Icons are the SVGs imported at 4x (`project.godot`, `importer_defaults`) and mipmapped at load.
- Sound: footsteps are never one per villager: a beat (eighth notes at 100 bpm; quarter notes at 8x, thinned at 2x and 4x) picks the 3 busiest 16 x 16-cell chunks on screen, from a pool of 12 voices placed at the chunk's screen position (2D panning), grass or dirt by the wear under the walkers, a ford splash when they wade; +-2 dB and +-50 cents, no file twice in a row, beats 2 and 4 3 dB lower. The river loop follows the share of the river in view and the zoom. A bell at each dawn (at most every 6 s), place / erase knocks, a low muted knock when a tool is refused, the theme at -14 dB with gaps of 2 to 6 minutes.
