# What got better and what did not, in my honest view

Written by the lead at the end of night 3. `make_closing.py` reads the two lists below: every line that starts with "- " under "## Better" and under "## Not better". Other lines are ignored.

## Better

- The screen is calm. Every kind of thing has one fixed place: the ledger top left, the shop top right, the bell bottom left, navigation bottom right, one message top centre, one line of guidance bottom centre. A check compares every state of the screen on eight window and interface sizes with the layout sheet: nothing overlaps, nothing is cut off, nothing changes place when something else appears.
- The shop is a list of rows with a price instead of twelve three-line cards. Locked purchases are named on their group's heading, finished ones leave, each row has its own icon, and a tooltip says what a purchase does, what it is worth and why it cannot be bought yet. Eighteen text sizes became four.
- Messages wait their turn. One banner at a time, one note at a time, none appearing in a single frame; about two thirds of the old notes are gone; a banner that is no longer true is dropped; a request says Done or Too late in its own place.
- The first minute explains itself with four short hints that show once.
- The town is better to look at: villagers are people with hats, hoods and arms; cottages come in nine shapes with chimneys and smoke; a real mill whose sails turn; five kinds of tree; fields that read as crops; a river with banks, reeds and shimmer; crossings and boats that can be read; the charter stone found at a glance.
- The book lies on a wooden table and has tooled leather; its turning leaf looks like a leaf; an interrupted animation keeps its speed; the pause question and the settings' boxes are one kind of card.
- How it plays is unchanged by the numbers: the whole first age played through real clicks and keys reaches the charter at minute 33.9 with 201 purchases, with the same purchases and decisions a minute as before the night.

## Not better

- Nobody has played this with their own hands or heard any of its sounds. Everything was judged from pictures, twelve-frame strips and measurements, on a machine without a graphics card.
- The second age was not played through by real input, as your step 6 asked "if there is time left": there was none. Villagers housed by the river still walk home to a first-age cottage, and the ford still matters on about half of the towns.
- There is no title page when the game starts.
- The animals are too small to see at the normal view; a reviewer wrote "the town is not yet alive". Loads are a few pixels there too and read by colour more than by shape.
- The gable-ended cottage reads dark from above, and every roof is the same thatch yellow.
- With many things affordable the shop is a wall of gold pills, and early in the game the ledger is mostly blank ruled paper.
- The age-change leaf reads as a leaf laid down and lifted again, not as one forward turn. Pressing Esc twice quickly still overshoots a little.
- Zoomed out, the page stage and the book stage now look almost alike: both show the whole book.
- The digits have a slashed zero (the interface font has no other); it reads as code in a book.
- The small rewards (the coin hop, dust, ripples, the bell's rings) were reviewed once and fixed once; I have not seen them move.
- Under load three tests are not steady (a row coming to rest in the town test, a ten-second wait in the settings test, the motion test's 6 ms gate): they pass on a quiet machine, but a steady test would not need one.
