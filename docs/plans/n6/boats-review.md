# Boats, landing and crane: review (reviewer, 27 November)

Judged from the twelve-frame strips only (after, with before beside them), then the target pictures, then the builder's report. Crops are in `build/n6-review/` (`after-boat-visit-n0/n1/c0/c1.jpg`, `after-boat-wait-*`, `before-*`). Frame names are the captions: "visit 4", "wait 8". N = normal view, C = close view. A still cannot show smoothness, speed, whether a bob or an oar stroke is right, or whether the crane's swing is eased. I say below where I am guessing.

## Short answer

The river itself is much better. The boat sails in the middle of the water, on a smooth curve, bow along the bend (visit 1, 2, 3 N). The big before faults (sideways jump in the bends, bow flick) show no sign of coming back. The before strip had a boat heading 20 degrees off its line in visit 2; now the bow follows. The quay, crane, yard and signs are a real step up in look from the jetty.

But the thing the owner will see first is still wrong: **boats lie on top of each other** (visit 4, 5, 7; wait 5, 6, 7, 8), and the waiting boats lie in the middle of the river instead of at posts out of the lanes. That is the design's own "nobody passes through anybody", and it fails in 6 of the 12 visit frames and 5 of 12 wait frames. The builder knows about visit 4 and 5. It is wider than that.

## 1. The journey

- On the water, bow along the course, one lane: yes, visit 1, 2, 3, 11 N. The wake is a V that is plain at speed (visit 3, wait 11) and drops away when slow. It fits.
- Bends: visit 1 to 2 shows a boat leaning into a bend with the bow tracking, no sideways step. Good. Two neighbouring frames cannot prove it is smooth between them.
- Coming alongside: weak. At visit 5 (t-0.4s) the rowboat is still a boat-length out in the river and at 6 (t+1.5s) it is "Moored" at the quay. In 1.9 s it has covered the S and stopped. Either it is quick, or it is the sim's berth and the drawn boat arrives late. Can't tell from stills. Wait 5 to 6 is the same: Arriving at +13.7 s, Moored at +14.4 s, 0.7 s apart, and in both the boat is lying at the south end of the quay, not at its berth.
- Lying moored: reads. Boat bow up, oars shipped, hold lid showing (visit 7, 8 N; wait 9 N).
- Going away: visit 9 to 10 is the best sequence: boat off the quay, bow swung across the stream at about 70 degrees (visit 10), then down the far lane (visit 11 shows two boats in two lanes, no overlap). That reads as a manoeuvre. At visit 10 the bow is turned so far the boat looks sideways-slipping, but it is a push-off, and fine.
- Wait 8 (t+15.9s): the barge pulls away from the quay while a rowboat that has not got its berth sits right beside it, and the gangplank lies across the water at a steep angle ending at the rowboat. See finding 5.
- Drawbridge: the boat sails to the stones and into them (visit 3, wait 1), and leaves over them (visit 12, wait 12). See finding 3.

## 2. Boats and each other

Overlaps, by frame:
- visit 4 N and C: the rowboat's bow and a barge's stern, sails and hold are one picture. The rowboat is inside the barge's hull.
- visit 5 N and C: same, a rowboat's oar and hull over the barge's side, with the barge's mast above them.
- visit 6 N: not overlapping, but the barge lies in the middle of the river 1.2 boat-lengths below the quay with the rowboat at the quay. It is out of the lane only by half a hull.
- visit 7 N and C: barge in mid river; the moored rowboat is beside it, nearly touching.
- wait 5 N: waiting rowboat under the barge's long sweep (oar), stern of the barge on its hull.
- wait 6 N and C, wait 7 N and C: the rowboat's bow lies under the barge's stern, and the barge's two sweeps (long grey poles, they read as spears or masts) cross the rowboat.
- wait 8 N and C: the gangplank (a long timber plank) lies over the rowboat, and the barge's sweep over its hull.
- Hull on the quay or bank: none that I can see. At low water (visit 9 to wait 12) boats run in a narrow ribbon of water between mud; wait 11 the rowboat's wake arm touches the mud. Fine, but it shows the river is much narrower than the lane maths suppose.

## 3. The landing

What works: the quay is a proper timber deck on piles, with bollards in a row along the edge, a hatch-shaped grey berth plate with a good icon, a crane at the north end, bales and carts in the yard, a little house at the foot. It reads as a place that works. It is the right scale beside the cottages (three cottage-widths long, deck about a boat-and-a-half wide).

What does not:
- **The sign is too small and too plain at the normal view.** At visit 5 N it is a 25 px grey plate with a white icon. I cannot see a bar or its colour. I cannot tell wool from cloth (wait 3 N: the icon is a grey keyhole-like shape, not the cloth icon I would expect), nor how full the boat is, nor how long it will stay. The target picture's signs were three times the size, white boards with a visible bar, on posts, facing the camera. The sign here lies flat on the deck and is the same colour as the quay's roof of stones. The builder says the bar is "thin"; at the normal view I see no bar.
- **There are no stacks you can name.** The design promises one stack per kind of good with the stacks showing what is ready. In the strips the yard is a heap of carts and bales to the west of the quay with villagers standing in a knot, the same as before. I can find no stack that reads as "wool" or "cloth". The target's neat stacks (planned picture, bottom right) are missing.
- **The treadwheel is not seen.** It was in the target as a big wooden wheel by the crane. In every frame I can see, the crane stands on the quay's north end and I find no wheel (visit 8 N shows the base under a heap of wool; the wheel may be hidden under it). That is also a place the builder's own rule fails: a wheel you can't see turn is not a loading you can see.
- **The mooring line is not visible at all** in any frame, N or C, in visit 6, 7, 8 or wait 9. The gangplank shows only once (wait 8) and then at a wrong angle (finding 5). So "line to the bollard, gangplank down" does not read.
- Villagers still stand in a knot at the yard (visit 9, 10; wait 3, 4: a cluster of six). The builder says this needs the villagers' files; it is the most "unfinished" look left of the landing.
- The rings: concentric white rings on the water at the crane (visit 5, 6, 7; wait 5, 6, 7), around a boat (visit 9) and in a figure of eight (wait 10). They read as a selection or a mooring marker, with no stated rule. They were in the old game too (before visit 5 shows the same). A player will ask what they are. They also look like the crane's hook splashing, which it is not.

## 4. The crane

- Can I see it load? Yes, in two frames: visit 8 N and C (jib turned toward the yard, a net full of wool over the heaps) and wait 8, wait 9 (jib leaning over the quay, hook at the boat). Visit 7 and 9 show the jib in two different places over the deck. So it moves with the loading and not on a clock, as far as stills can say. Can't tell whether it ever swings empty; I saw no frame with an empty net swinging.
- Idle or busy for nothing? visit 8 (t+149.8s): the boat at the berth is "0/601 Wool" and the crane is lifting a full net, the jib over the yard. Either it is loading the barge that lies in the river (the barge's hold is full of wool, visit 7) or it is lifting for a boat that is about to leave empty. I can't tell, and the player can't either. If it is the barge, the crane is working a boat that is not at a berth. Check this.
- It leaves boats at 0 after 150 s: the whole visit strip (visit 6 to 12) shows a rowboat for wool that gets 0 of 601 and leaves. That may be the right rule (the town had no wool; the boat gave up) but it makes the strip a poor demonstration of loading: the leaving boat rides high (visit 9, 10, hull light and the hold empty), which does show "gave up".
- Look and ink rule: the solid jib and the base read as the same family as the cottages: dark ink outline, flat wood. Seen from above the jib is a plain timber beam, clearly solid. I cannot count the treads (no wheel visible), so I cannot judge "six treads".

## 5. The boats themselves

- Oars: read well on the rowboat, spread wide at speed (visit 1, 3; wait 11) and trailing when moored (visit 6). The barge's two sweeps read as long grey poles, thin, no blade: they look like spears or poles, especially wait 6 to 8 where they are crossed by the rowboat.
- People: the rower at the oars is clear. The barge's steersman is clear.
- Cargo: red crates in the cloth barge (wait 3 to 8) are very clear; white bales in the wool barge (visit 5 to 7) are clear. A rowboat's hold shows a slatted lid when empty (visit 7 C). Good.
- Pennant: I see none, at visit 12 or wait 10, 11, 12 where the rowboat leaves with 120/120. Not read. If it exists it is not visible from above, because a pennant on a mast from above is a line.
- Riding low when loaded: wait 11 C vs visit 11 C: the loaded rowboat does sit lower; the red crates are visible and the freeboard is smaller. It is subtle but it is there. At the normal view I would not see it.
- The rowboat that leaves full carries visible crates in its hold (wait 11 N). That reads.

## 6. Against the target pictures

Promised and missing: large signs with a bar; neat labelled stacks; the treadwheel by the crane; the quay long enough for two berths side by side *with a boat on each* (the game has the quay and two berths, but at visit 6 and wait 3 only one has a boat on it and the other is taken by a boat in the middle of the river); the line of villagers; the mooring line and gangplank in use; the planned pic's rowboat floating clear of the lanes.

The game does better than the mockup: the water level changes with the tide (low water draws the mud and the narrow channel, visit 9 on), the wake V is better than the mock's, the boats' oars move, and the crane and quay are drawn in the game's real ink style, where the mock was a staged still.

## 7. Rules that do not exist, or hidden ones

- **The waiting boat looks stuck, and lies in the fairway.** Wait 3 and 4 (6.6 s and 12.9 s): the waiting rowboat sits in the middle of the river, bow up, with no post, no line, no sign of why. The design said "at a mooring post, bow into the stream". There is no post in the picture. A player reads this as a boat that has stopped for no reason. The barge ahead of it is at the quay, so the reason is a full berth, but nothing says so.
- Wait 1, visit 3: the boat sails to the stones and the translucent block of the crossing. The label "The drawbridge, at level 27" appears at visit 4 and wait 2 on the river a few cells below the bridge. The picture does not say whether the bridge is open or down; at visit 12, wait 12 the boat lies *over* the stones. If it is a bridge that is up or open, the stones should not be there. If the stones are the bridge down, the boat is under it. Either it is a fault or the picture hides the state. I cannot decide from the stills. This reads as a boat passing through a crossing.
- The river at low water (visit 9 to 12) shows a main channel of about two cells in a mud bed of six. The lanes are "as far apart as the river allows", but in a channel of two cells there are not two lanes. At visit 11 N the two boats lie 1.5 cells apart, side by side, with one on the mud edge. A rule "one lane at low water" would be believable; what the picture shows is two lanes in a channel that is too narrow for them.
- The sign's colour for time left: the player is told nothing about it and cannot read it. The old text said "Leaving, Wool 0 of 121". Nothing in the after pictures says a boat is leaving, except it moves. The ledger holds it, but the owner asked for use-wise better.

## 8. Findings, worst first

**Must fix**

1. **Two boats on top of each other** (visit 4 N/C, visit 5 N/C, wait 5, 6, 7, 8 N/C). Where: the rowboat lies inside the barge's hull and sweeps. Wrong: the first thing the design promised was that nobody passes through anybody. The builder's report puts it down to the simulation holding both at the same place. The player does not care why. Do instead: the draw layer takes the whole queue of boats below the berth as one line, each a hull length behind the one ahead, whatever the simulation says; a boat that must wait for a berth stops *behind* the last one, never beside or through. Add the frames to game-boats' watch (the check passes on a run that does not meet them).
2. **Waiting boats lie in the middle of the river, with no post** (wait 3, 4 N; visit 6, 7 N for the barge). Wrong: it reads as a stuck boat, and it blocks the lane the next boat comes by, which is the very cause of finding 1. Do instead: a visible post (pile with a ring) at each waiting place, on the near side below the quay, and the boat lying against it bow into the stream. If there is no room, a boat waits behind the boat ahead, in a line, as above.
3. **Boat on the stepping stones / crossing** (visit 12 N/C, wait 12 N/C; wait 1 N/C, visit 3 N/C). Where: the boat's hull is drawn over the stone row and its pale block. Wrong: reads as a boat passing over a crossing. Do instead: decide what the stones are; if the bridge is open to boats, do not draw the stones across the fairway while it is open (draw the bridge raised), and if it is down, hold the boat *in front of* it, with the bridge label naming why ("waits for the bridge").

**Should fix**

4. **Signs too small to read at the normal view** (visit 5 N, wait 3 N). A grey plate of about 25 px with a white icon; bar not seen. Wrong: the design's "readable from the normal view" fails; I cannot tell what the boat wants or how full it is. Do instead: a board on a post at the quay's edge, standing up facing the camera, three times as large, the bar a full-width strip in the time colour, as in the target picture. Put the amount on it in figures when the camera is close.
5. **Gangplank shown wrong, mooring line never shown** (wait 8 N/C: a long timber plank from the quay's corner across open water to a rowboat, steeper than the boat's length; every other frame has none). Wrong: reads as a pole or a ramp over the water; lying mooring is not shown as tied. Do instead: the plank short, from the quay's edge to the gunwale, only while the boat is alongside and loading; one rope from bow to bollard that you can see in visit 6, 7, 8 (a dark line with a sag).
6. **The crane may be working a boat that is not at a berth, or none** (visit 8 N/C: net lifted while the berthed boat shows 0/601 and leaves). Wrong: "busy with nothing to load". Do instead: let the crane load only boats lying at a berth; if it loads the barge waiting out in the river, bring the barge to the berth first. Show in a strip a berthed boat being filled, lift by lift, so the reviewer can see the hold fill.
7. **The treadwheel is not visible** (every frame, the crane area). Wrong: the promised "wheel turns while it works and stands still when idle" cannot be seen, and the crane loses its tell. Do instead: put the wheel where the target had it, beside the base on the land side, clear of the stacks, so that one glance says "works / idle".
8. **No stacks in the yard** (visit 8 N, wait 3 N). Wrong: no way to tell what is ready to go aboard; the heap of bales is a heap. Do instead: one low stack per good in its own bay on the deck behind the quay, in the good's colour, so the sign (4) and the stack (this) say the same thing.
9. **Two berths, one used.** At visit 6 and 7 the barge waits in the river with a free berth beside the rowboat... the free berth is the north one under the crane (visit 6 N). Wrong: reads as a rule "one boat at a time" that does not exist. Do instead: take the barge to the free berth when one is free, or leave one berth visibly marked "not yet" (a chain, a tape). (Cannot tell if the sim gives the berth to the barge; if not, say it on the barge's sign.)
10. **The rings on the water** (visit 5, 6, 7, 9; wait 5, 6, 7, 10) are unexplained. A mark that turns up around a boat, at the crane, and in a figure of eight has no meaning for the player. Do instead: either make them a visible thing (a buoy at each berth's waiting place, the ring drawn once and still) or remove them from boats not selected.

**Could fix**

11. The barge's sweeps are thin grey poles with no blade; they read as spears (wait 6, 7, 8). A flat blade at the end would make them oars.
12. Pennant on a full boat is not visible from above (wait 10 to 12). Make it stream out sideways and give it a colour that stands out from the deck.
13. Villagers wait in a knot at the yard (visit 9, 10; wait 3, 4). The builder says it needs the villagers' own files. Say who does it and when; as it is, the landing's biggest "use" change is not there.
14. The camera is not centred on the boat (visit 1 N and wait 1 N: the boat sits left of centre). This is the strip tool, not the game.
15. The "The drawbridge, at level 27" label lies in the river just below the bridge in a lot of frames (visit 4, 5, 11; wait 2, 3, 4, 5...) and reads as water text. The old text over the water was meant to go. Move it to the bridge.

## 9. Verdict

Do the boats still "move terrible"? Alone on the river: no. A single boat on a bend, coming in, lying and going away, in the frames I have, looks like a boat. With two boats at the landing: yes, it still looks bad, because they lie in each other, and a boat that waits looks stuck in the middle of the river. The design's best idea, lanes and posts so nobody passes through anybody, is the part that is not there in the picture.

The one change that does the most: **the drawn boats must queue** (finding 1 and 2 together): each boat below the berth is drawn a hull length behind the one ahead, and a boat waiting for a berth lies at a visible post. With that done, six of my fifteen findings (the overlaps, the stuck-looking boat, the sweeps crossing, the plank over a boat, the barge in the lane, the "one at a time" look) go away.

I have not run the game: where I say "can't tell", the strip's twelve frames for 90 to 160 s of game time do not show it. The builder's report names visit 4 and 5, the sign's thin bar, no villager line, and the camera; I found it more widespread on the overlaps (it lists two frames, I find twelve), and I found it did not mention the stones, the missing treadwheel, stacks, line, gangplank or the rings.
