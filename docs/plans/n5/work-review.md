# Work you can watch: review from pictures

Reviewer: did not build it. Judged from the strips, the single frames and the staged board only. Crops are in `/home/user/tide-toll/build/n5-review/` (`after-<job>-normal1|2.jpg` and `-close1|2.jpg`, frames 1-4 and 5-8; same for `before-`). Frame paths below: strips are `docs/plans/n5/strips/after/work-<job>.jpg`, singles are `docs/plans/n5/after/<place>-<view>.jpg`.

Caveat: stills cannot show a swing, a turning crank or a puff in motion. Where I say "cannot be seen" I mean in a still at that frame.

## 1. Does each job read as a scene?

**Reaping: yes, the best of the five, at the normal view.** Line: frames 1-2 (`after-reap-normal1.jpg`) show eight or nine villagers strung along the lane toward the field, clearly a queue. Work: frames 3-7 show one or two bent figures at the west end of the field with a brown cut strip behind them. A stranger would say "they are cutting the field". Goods: a small stook stands at the end of the cut row (frames 3-6, right end of the strip) and the worker walks off with grain (frame 7). The sickle swing is not readable at the normal view, which is fine. Close view (`after-reap-close1.jpg`) frames 3-4 read the same, no better.

**Water: partly.** Line: frames 1-2 show people near the well, but the "line" is two or three bodies stacked head over head at the well's roof edge (`after-water-normal1.jpg`, frame 1 at the roof, frame 4 three heads piled at the top). It looks like a knot, not a line. Work: frames 3-4: the worker is half under the roof and the crank, rope and bucket cannot be seen; the only answer is a thin blue ring on the grass beside the well. Goods: frame 7 shows someone walking with pails, but the pails on the rim are visible only in `well-close.jpg` and not as a taking beat. Reads as "someone is at the well", not as drawing water.

**Mill: weak.** Line: frames 1-2 (`after-mill-normal1.jpg`) a line stands on the lane north of the mill, readable. Work: the worker is at the north-east side, behind the sails (frames 3-5). The sails do turn in the frames, but the door, the flour puff and the worker are hidden by the sails and the roof. Goods: the flour sack on the step is visible only in `mill-close.jpg`; at the normal view frames 6-8 a villager walks away carrying "Flour" that looks the same as grain.

**Market: partly.** Line: frames 1-8 (`after-market-normal1.jpg`) show a long queue along the lane, and it reads as a queue. Work and answer: the table is at the far end behind the awning (`market-close.jpg`), goods are on the left edge of the table and a stallholder stands behind it. At the normal view goods and customer are a dozen pixels, not readable. Goods: a large pale yellow disc hangs in the air by the lane (frames 1, 2, 7, 8) and is carried on heads; I cannot tell what it is.

**Coming home: not readable at the normal view.** The cottage is far from the well and the villager is small (`after-home-normal1.jpg`). The door opens (green door ajar in `cottage-close.jpg`, good) and the chimney puffs, but at the normal view the door is three pixels. Frame 8 shows nothing at the cottage: the villager has gone inside and the strip loses them. "Steps out with empty pails" is not shown.

## 2. What is wrong or ugly, ranked

### Must fix (reads as a fault)

1. **The mill hides its own answer, and the work is on the wrong side.** `strips/after/work-mill.jpg`, frames 3-5, and `after/mill-close.jpg`. The queue and the worker use the north-east corner under the sails; the door and step are on the south face where nobody stands. The door opening and the flour puff cannot be seen, and the villager is half behind the sail frame. Move the work spot to the door side (as the board did) or move the door and the puff to the side the lane reaches.
2. **A crouching figure at the mill looks fallen.** `after/mill-close.jpg`, right of the tower around (860, 400): a yellow villager is bent double with a sack, one arm folded, with a tiny hexagon floating beside it. At normal view it is a yellow lump. Make the stoop shallower, keep the head and shoulders upright, and put the sack on the step.
3. **Dark T-shaped smear at the field's west end.** `after-reap-normal1.jpg`, frames 3-6, around (300-330, 940) in frame 3 and `after-reap-close1.jpg` frames 3-4 around (455, 820). A dark cross of sticks stands at the row end, with the worker half on it. It reads as a cart or a broken scarecrow standing in the crop. If it is the tool or basket, colour it like the sheaf (gold and brown), not near-black, and keep it off the soil.
4. **The well's worker and queue are hidden under the roof.** `after-water-normal1.jpg` frames 3-4; `after/well-close.jpg`. The roof covers the rope, crank, bucket and half the worker; two villagers stack head over head at the roof edge. The place's answer (the rising bucket) is invisible. Put the worker on the open lane side, lift the bucket out beyond the roof edge, and spread the line along the lane a body-length apart.

### Should fix (reads as unfinished)

5. **The field is too dark and too busy beside the town.** `after/field-1-close.jpg`, `after/field-2-normal.jpg`. The dark brown soil frame is the darkest big shape on the map; the rows are chunky hedge-like blocks with heavy ink on every leaf, so they read as bushes. The cottages, mill and well are flat and calm. It does fit the low-poly style, but not the calm: thin the outlines on the leaves, lighten the soil one step towards the path colour, make the stalks smaller and fewer per row. It is better than the flat yellow slab the owner called ugly, but it is now the loudest thing on the map.
6. **Cut strips are cluttered with debris and read as bare earth.** `after/field-3-close.jpg`, lower left: white sticks, dark dots, three little stooks and a straw hat shape lie in one cut strip. At the normal view (`after-reap-normal2.jpg`, frame 8) the cut strips are two dark brown stripes with no stubble colour. The lead's rule says a field must never look bare. Give the cut strip a visible pale stubble tone and drop the loose white sticks.
7. **A worker is buried in the crop.** `after/field-3-close.jpg` around (765, 540): only a round brown head shows in the rows. `after-reap-normal1.jpg` frame 4, around (1360, 855), a dark shape in the crop reads as fallen. Raise the figure over the row top, or lower the rows next to a worker.
8. **The market's disc floats.** `after-market-normal1.jpg` frames 1, 2 (large pale disc above the lane near (440, 190) and (1405, 195)) and `after/market-close.jpg` (upper left). It hangs in the air away from any hand or table. Either it is a hopping coin or goods in flight; either way it is far too large for a coin and unreadable as goods. Shrink it, give it a shadow, and only draw it above the table or a hand.
9. **The queues look doubled and longer than they are.** `after-market-normal1.jpg`, `after-reap-close1.jpg` frames 1-2, `after-mill-normal2.jpg`. Villagers stand two abreast in places (pairs with overlapping heads), and walkers carrying goods on the same lane look like part of the line. The market line looks like twelve people; the lane is shared by field, mill and market, so one line can look like it belongs to another place. Keep one body per step, put the line on one side of the lane, and give waiters a distinct pose (facing the place, hands empty).
10. **Walkers with pails are dark blobs.** `after-water-normal1.jpg` frame 1 at (665, 160), frame 2 at (1530, 195) and (1285, 190): yoke and pails are one near-black navy lump beside the body, so it is not clear if it is a person or a barrel. Lighten the pails to the well's blue and keep the yoke thin.
11. **The mill's goods and the market's goods cannot be told apart at the normal view.** Grain, flour and water carried look alike (`after-mill-normal2.jpg` frames 6-8). Use three clear silhouettes and colours: a pale sack for flour, a gold sheaf for grain, blue pails for water.

### Could fix (taste)

12. **The "close" row is barely closer than the normal row.** `strips/after/work-water.jpg`, bottom rows. Frame 8 close is a different part of the map. The strip is fine for review but does not prove the fine detail (swing, crank).
13. **The well's wet ring is a thin blue outline off-centre on the grass.** `after/well-close.jpg` at (575, 610). It reads as a stray halo, not wet ground. The board's brown wet earth was better.
14. **The cottage door is the right idea but too small.** `after/cottage-close.jpg`. At the normal view, nothing says a villager entered. Add a brief lit window or a bigger door swing; the chimney puff is good.
15. **Small UI labels sit on the scene.** `after-reap-normal1.jpg`: the "+ hand" plate sits on the field's top edge in every frame, and "A cottage, at level 14" plates cover the lane. Not the feature's fault, but they sit on the one place the player is meant to watch.

Fine as it is: the cut and regrow of field rows (three states visible, green rows are lovely), the mill sails turning at different angles, the cottage door and chimney, the lined-up queue at the field.

## 3. Against the staged board (`mockup/work-now-and-planned.png`)

The board promised, and the game does not show:
- A queue on a worn dirt path, one body per step, each person facing the place. The game queues on the paved lane, in pairs.
- A dark wet earth ring round the well with pails on the rim and the worker at the open side; in the game the roof hides it.
- The mill with its door open and the worker in the doorway among sacks; in the game the worker is behind the sails and the door is empty.
- A calm field: thin, tidy rows in four colours, a scarecrow, a handcart. The game's field is denser, darker and louder.
- A market customer at the front of the table with the goods large and clear; in the game, a tiny figure and a floating disc.

The game does better: the field's cut strips grow back in three real states with several workers at once; the queue is real and long, not three staged figures; the sails are really moving; the cottage's door actually opens. It is also a living town, not a posed still.

## 4. Rules that do not exist but are suggested

- The cut brown stripes in the field look like the field is being used up (`after-reap-normal2.jpg` frame 8, two dark stripes and no stubble colour). Keep them pale.
- The market line looks like twelve waiting for the stall, but it shares the lane with grain carriers to the mill; players will read all of them as one queue.
- A single dark T at the field's end looks like a cart that only appears at harvest.

## 5. Ranking

Must fix: 1, 2, 3, 4. Should fix: 5-11. Could fix: 12-15.

## 6. Verdict

Not "super basic" any more, but not finished. Reaping is a scene now; the market and the mill are half a scene and the well and the home are barely one at the normal view. The beats exist in the simulation; the pictures hide them behind roofs, sails and awnings, and several props (the T, the disc, the dark pails) read as mistakes. The single change that would do the most: **move every work spot to the open front of its place (the mill's door side, the well's lane side, the market's table front) and put the line on one side of the lane, one body per step.** Then everything else (door, puff, bucket, goods) becomes visible without any new art. Second: calm the field's colour and outlines so it sits with the cottages.
